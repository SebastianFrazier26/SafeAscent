SafeAscent

Route-level risk estimates for climbers. For a route and a month, SafeAscent estimates how its rate of reported incidents compares with routes of the same type nationally. It borrows evidence from nearby routes and from characteristically similar routes anywhere, and shows the result as a 0–100 rank with a likely range and a confidence level.

Revival in progress. safeascent.us is offline while the data pipeline, model, and operations are rebuilt, and the app is being relaunched. The code is public and changing week to week. No score shown before the rebuild should be relied on.

What it shows

A rank among routes of the same type

The number is a percentile: where a route's estimated incident rate falls among all routes of its type (sport, trad, alpine, ice, mixed) for that month, nationally. The same four bands are used everywhere, each including its lower edge:

Lower0 – 25
Moderate25 – 50
Elevated50 – 75
High75 – 100

The lowest band reads Lower than typical for this type, in neutral slate. It is a rank, not a verdict, and never says the route is safe.

Never a made-up number

Low confidence still shows the number with its likely range, for example “40.0 (likely 20.0–60.0)”, hatched.
Not enough evidence (gray, no number) only when there are no reported incidents on the route, nearby, or on similar routes to borrow from. No local incidents alone is never read as safe.
Unavailable (a dash, no colour) when there is no stored score for the route. It never falls back to 0.

How the estimate works

Reported incidents on any one route are rare, so a route's own count says little on its own. The model compares what was reported with what would be expected from how much a route is climbed and what it is like, then pools that comparison with other routes in proportion to how much evidence each has.

  • Expected incidents. A Poisson regression on exposure (estimated climbing traffic) and static route features (terrain, elevation, climate normals, type and month) gives the number of incidents a route would see at the national rate for its type, μ₀.
  • Borrowing from nearby and similar routes. Empirical-Bayes shrinkage up the area hierarchy (area, region, state, national) gives a geographic estimate. A second estimate comes from routes with similar characteristics anywhere (a feature archetype for the same type). The two are blended by how precise each is, and the blend's spread includes how much they disagree.
  • The route's own record. The route's posterior rate is a Gamma distribution: its own incidents count against μ₀, the blend acts as a prior worth κ expected incidents. A route with little traffic leans on the prior; a busy route with its own history leans on that history.
  • Ratio, rank and range. The rate is divided by the national rate for the type and month (the relative rate, “1.4× the national rate”), then ranked among all routes of that type. The 90% interval gives the likely range of both.
  • Confidence. Set by how wide the interval is (the ratio of its upper to lower end, with cutoffs chosen per model version on held-out data) and capped by evidence: “High” also needs at least 10 effective incidents behind the estimate, including at least one on the route or 5 nearby.
posterior rate  θ ~ Gamma(y_route + κ·m,  μ₀ + κ)     m = blended prior mean
relative rate   = θ / national rate for the type and month
score           = percentile of the relative rate among routes of the same type

Every model version is trained offline, backtested against held-out incidents, and promoted only when it beats the version it replaces. Weather forecasts are not part of the served score yet.

Made-up inputs, not a real route. This toy runs the model's shrinkage, interval, banding and confidence steps on numbers you choose. Its fixed constants (band edges, interval quantiles, evidence gates, the prior-shape floor) are the backend's own and are checked against it in CI. The fitted parts are stand-ins: the prior strengths κ, the overdispersion φ and the confidence cutoffs are fixed example values here, while the real model fits them per version. The geographic chain is one level instead of three, and the rank is taken against a synthetic population of routes. It does not reproduce any score the app shows.

Architecture

Hosted on Railway (frontend, API, worker, beat, Redis) with Postgres on Neon. Scores are computed when a model version is trained, never at request time; the API serves only the active version's stored rows. The API, data jobs and training each connect with their own least-privilege role. Missed nightly checks page through healthchecks.io dead-man checks.

Data and rules

  • Incidents: American Alpine Club, Avalanche.org/CAIC and NPS records, cleaned and de-duplicated by audited repair jobs. Not redistributed.
  • Exposure and features: a composite climbing-traffic estimate, terrain and elevation from USGS 3DEP, and ERA5 climate normals keyed to each crag's local calendar day.
  • Licensing: code is Apache-2.0; data follows DATA_LICENSE.md. CI guards block scraper code anywhere in the repository and third-party route identifiers in its data, docs and test fixtures.
